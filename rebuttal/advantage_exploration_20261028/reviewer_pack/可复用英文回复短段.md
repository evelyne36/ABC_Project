## t7wg: main contribution and prior work

We agree that linear leaves, fitting-aware partition evaluation, and univariate model selection have substantial prior history. We will make the contribution specific: CrossGB combines a constant-or-single-slope weak learner, predictive-feature candidates that need not belong to the routing path, predictive-aware histogram split scoring, and independent terminal-leaf feature selection within second-order boosting. PL Trees already use linear-leaf fitting in tree construction, and PILOT already selects univariate node models. Our contribution is this particular formulation and training combination. The fixed-backbone four-way ablation tests its conditional benefits and also retains mechanisms where the full combination is worse. We will explicitly distinguish TAO's linear-leaf alternating optimization and CLARITree's sparse lookahead optimization. We do not claim a new principle of linear model trees or superiority over these recent methods without direct evidence.

**Evidence:** original reviews pp.5–6; analysis_update/README §3; official [PL Trees](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2019/476), [PILOT](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.03931v1), [TAO2025](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/c023f4ec4c567ad48188e9b2ce6bdba7-Abstract-Conference.html), [CLARITree2026](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/wang26fu.html). ## Shared: why one predictive feature, and what N5 tests

The single-feature restriction is both a low-capacity inductive bias and a computational design choice; it is not intended to be optimal for every local response. We examined 35 fixed-routing capacity reports across seven settings, comparing slope-only and affine refits with one, two, or three predictors. Dense S1 and protein benefited from additional predictors, and the regional-intercept task benefited from affine models. These opposing outcomes support a capacity trade-off. This probe does not replace joint retraining, retuning, or an all-feature native CrossGB comparison.
